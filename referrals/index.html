How Our Referrals Work

Owners Inc. sometimes receives a referral fee when we connect a homeowner with a local contractor. Here is exactly how that works, in plain words.

Owners Inc. Everybody's doing it.

The short version: When we connect a homeowner with a local contractor and the homeowner hires them, the contractor sometimes pays us a referral fee. The homeowner never pays it, it never adds to the price of the job, and we only ever recommend contractors we would name anyway.

Why we get asked

Owners Inc. does bookkeeping and simple web work for small businesses around Hastings, Minnesota. Because of that, homeowners and shop owners ask us all the time: "Who would you call for a roof? A furnace? A remodel?" We answer those questions either way. This page exists so you know, up front and in writing, that some of those answers come with a fee paid to us by the contractor.

What the fee is

  • The contractor pays us a percentage of what the customer actually paid for the finished job — between 5% and 10% depending on the trade.
  • It is paid only after the job is done and paid for. If you don't hire them, or the job never happens, nobody pays anything.
  • It comes out of the contractor's side, like their advertising does. Contractors who work with us agree in writing that a referred customer gets the same pricing as any other customer for the same work.
  • There is no charge to you for an introduction, ever.

What it never changes

  • We recommend contractors because of their work and their name in this area — the fee arrangements are signed with businesses we were already comfortable naming.
  • You are always free to get other quotes, and you should. A referral from us is a starting point, not a decision.
  • Your contract is with the contractor. We don't do the work, supervise it, set its price, or guarantee it — licensing, permits, warranties, and the work itself are the contractor's.
  • If a contractor's work goes wrong, tell us. A referral relationship is not worth more than our name, and we pause referrals when a problem is reported.

Where we never take a fee

Some kinds of referrals are legally off-limits for fees, and we don't take them — in either direction, from anyone:

  • Mortgage, title, closing, or other real-estate settlement services (federal law — RESPA — prohibits referral fees there).
  • Healthcare services (anti-kickback law).
  • Legal services (lawyers may not share fees with non-lawyers).
  • Insurance products or agent services (that compensation requires an insurance license).

If you ask us for a name in one of those areas, we'll give you one as a plain courtesy, with nothing attached to it.
