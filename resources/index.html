Help for Right Now — and for Digging Out

If you are in a sober house, a shelter, a car, or a doorway tonight, this page is for you. Every number here is free to call. You do not need insurance, an ID, or money to make most of these calls.

Start at the top if things are bad right now. Start at the ladder if today is steady and you want the next step up. One call at a time is fine. One call is how it starts.

Find Help Fast

Don’t know which door is yours? Start with 211. A real person looks up what is open near you — shelter beds, food, rent help, rides — and tells you the next step.

Call 211 Free, private, 24/7 — or text your ZIP code to 898211 211.org — search online

Looking for treatment?

FindTreatment.gov — treatment near you FastTrackerMN.org — MN programs & openings

Minnesota’s Fast-Tracker shows mental health and substance-use programs across the state, with openings kept up to date by the programs themselves. If you have no insurance, ask about the Behavioral Health Fund when you call a program — it can pay for an assessment and treatment for people who qualify.

Dakota County’s own crisis team answers around the clock:

952-891-7171 Dakota County Crisis Response — 24/7

Near Here — Minnesota & Dakota County

Dakota County — Need a bed tonight?

651-554-5751 Housing Crisis / Resource Line

This is the front door for emergency shelter in Dakota County. For longer-term housing help, the county uses something called Coordinated Entry — one assessment that puts you on the list for housing programs instead of you calling every place yourself. If you don’t have a case manager, the Housing Crisis Line can point you to who does the assessment. Adults and families can also call Ally Supportive Services at 612-875-9683.

Dakota County — Cash, food & emergency help

651-554-5611 Employment & Economic Assistance

Emergency Assistance can help with rent or a utility shutoff — generally once in 12 months, and the county decides who qualifies. The same office handles food support and cash programs. You can also apply online at MNbenefits.mn.gov.

Hastings Family Service — Hastings, MN

651-437-7134 301 2nd St E, Hastings — call for an appointment

Food shelf (open to anyone), plus emergency help for people in the Hastings school district area: clothing and household vouchers, help with rent, utilities, prescriptions, and short-term crisis aid. Hours are Monday–Friday, 9–4. Bring a photo ID and a recent piece of mail if you have them — if you don’t, call anyway and ask what they can do.

hastingsfamilyservice.org

Bridging — Furniture for a New Place

952-888-1105 Bridging — questions line

When you land a place and it’s empty, Bridging furnishes it — beds, table, couch, dishes, linens. The honest rules: you can’t book it yourself. A case manager or agency worker has to refer you, so ask your worker, shelter, clinic, or county worker, “Can you refer me to Bridging?” There is an $80 appointment fee (your referring agency may help with it) and delivery is $230 more, Twin Cities metro only — picking up yourself is free if you can line up a truck. It’s for setting up a whole home, not just one or two items.

bridging.org — how it works

Outside Dakota County? Call 211 — every Minnesota county has its own crisis line, shelter door, and emergency assistance office, and 211 knows all of them.

The Dig-Out Ladder

Nobody climbs this in a day. Pick the rung that’s in front of you. Each one makes the next one easier.

Step 1Food

Food today

Food shelves don’t need an appointment at most places and don’t care about your story. Call 211 for the closest one, or search:

hungersolutions.org — find a food shelf

Food every month — SNAP (food support)

SNAP puts money for groceries on an EBT card each month. In Minnesota you apply once, online, in about 20 minutes — the same application can cover cash and emergency help too:

Apply at MNbenefits.mn.gov

Need food fast? Ask about expedited SNAP — people in an emergency can get benefits in 7 days or less. Regular applications can take up to 30 days, so applying early matters. Free help filling it out: Minnesota Food HelpLine, 1-888-711-1151.

Step 2ID & Papers

Almost everything on this ladder asks for ID. If yours is gone, here’s the order that works: birth certificate first, then Social Security card, then state ID. Each one helps you get the next.

  • Birth certificate (Minnesota births): $26 for a certified copy at any county vital records office, or by mail from the Minnesota Department of Health. Free if you are a homeless youth age 24 or younger. health.mn.gov — birth records
  • Social Security card: replacement cards are free — never pay a website for one. Start at ssa.gov or call 1-800-772-1213. If you know your number, that’s often enough — most employers and offices just need the number.
  • State ID or driver’s license: a replacement (duplicate) license or ID card costs $26 at a Minnesota driver’s license office — done in person, and you walk out with a temporary paper card. dps.mn.gov — fee list

Short on the fee? Ask at Hastings Family Service (651-437-7134) or call 211 and ask about ID fee help — some local programs cover exactly this, because they know everything else waits on it.

Step 3Health

Medical Assistance (MA) is Minnesota’s free health coverage for people with low incomes — it covers doctor visits, prescriptions, mental health, and substance-use treatment. MinnesotaCare is the low-cost option if your income is a bit higher. One application checks both:

Apply at MNsure.org 1-855-366-7873 MNsure help line — free help applying

MA can pay medical bills from up to three months before the month you apply — another reason not to wait. Free in-person help (“navigators”) exists in most towns; the help line can find yours.

Not a crisis, but your head won’t quiet down? The Minnesota Warmline is peer support — people who’ve been there, not clinicians: 651-288-0400, or text “support” to 85511.

Step 4Money & Benefits

  • Emergency Assistance: one-time help with rent or utilities so you don’t lose housing. Apply at MNbenefits.mn.gov or through your county — Dakota County: 651-554-5611.
  • General Assistance (GA): a small monthly cash benefit for single adults who can’t work or are waiting on disability decisions. Apply through your county or MNbenefits.
  • SSI / SSDI (disability): if a disability keeps you from working, apply with Social Security at ssa.gov or 1-800-772-1213. Denials the first time are common — that’s the system, not you. Appealing is normal, and free legal help (Step 7) can take it on.
  • Energy help: the Energy Assistance Program helps with heating bills. Dakota County area: CAP Agency, 651-322-3500.

One honest note: benefits have rules, waiting periods, and paperwork. A county worker or a 211 specialist can tell you in one call which of these you actually qualify for. That call is worth more than a week of guessing.

Step 5Work

CareerForce is Minnesota’s free job service — job listings, resume help, training programs, and people whose whole job is helping you get hired. No fee, ever. The location serving Dakota County is in Burnsville:

952-703-3100 CareerForce Burnsville — serves Dakota County careerforcemn.com — all locations & jobs

No email address yet? A free one (Gmail or Outlook) takes ten minutes at a library computer, and most shelters and CareerForce offices will help you set one up. A working phone number and an email address are the two tools every application asks for.

Step 6Housing

Housing programs in Minnesota mostly run through Coordinated Entry — one shared list per region. You do an assessment once, and programs pull names from that list as openings come up. It can be slow. Getting on the list early is the whole game, because time on the list counts.

  • Dakota County: start with the Housing Crisis Line, 651-554-5751. If you don’t have a case manager, they’ll connect you to who does assessments (adults & families: Ally Supportive Services, 612-875-9683; youth 24 and under: The Link, 612-205-7944).
  • Anywhere else in Minnesota: call 211 and say “Coordinated Entry.” They’ll give you your region’s access point.
  • Sober housing: ask your treatment program, your county chemical health worker (Dakota County: 651-554-6424), or 211 for sober and transitional housing openings near you. Oxford House’s site lists self-run sober homes with openings at oxfordhouse.org.
  • Deposit help: Emergency Assistance (Step 4) and local nonprofits sometimes cover a deposit or first month. Ask the Housing Crisis Line what’s open now — these funds come and go by the month.

Step 7Legal Help

Old warrants, evictions on your record, child support, a record that kills every job application — legal aid handles civil (non-criminal) problems for free if your income is low.

LawHelpMN.org — free legal help finder 1-877-696-6529 SMRLS — legal aid for southern MN, incl. Dakota County

Southern Minnesota Regional Legal Services (SMRLS) covers housing, benefits, family, and expungement cases in this part of the state. Expungement — sealing an old record — is worth asking about by name; it can change what landlords and employers see.

Step 8Recovery Support

Meetings are free, they’re every day, and nobody needs your last name. Pick the flavor that fits you — they all have online meetings too:

aa.org — find an A.A. meeting na.org/meetingsearch — find an N.A. meeting smartrecovery.org — SMART Recovery (no-steps approach)

The free Meeting Guide app (the blue folding-chair icon) lists A.A. meetings near you by time — handy when a craving hits at a random hour. For family and friends carrying this too: al-anon.org.

Minnesota also has peer recovery specialists — people in recovery trained to walk with you through exactly this. Ask any treatment program, county worker, or call the SAMHSA line (1-800-662-4357) and ask for peer support near you.

Step 9Phone & Internet

Lifeline is a federal program that knocks up to $9.25 a month off phone or internet service. If you get SNAP, Medical Assistance, SSI, or housing assistance, you likely already qualify. Some phone companies turn it into a free phone and free monthly service. Apply at:

lifelinesupport.org — apply for Lifeline

Straight talk: the bigger internet discount program (ACP) ended in 2024. If a website offers to sign you up for ACP, it’s out of date or a scam. Lifeline is the real one that’s still running.

Libraries are the other answer: free computers, free Wi-Fi, free printing for applications, and staff who help people with exactly this every day. A library card is free and it’s the best office you don’t have to pay for.

One Call at a Time

Nobody digs out in a day, and nobody does it in the right order. You make one call. You fill out one form. You show up to one meeting. Then you do the next one.

If a door closes, that’s one door — this page is full of doors. If a worker tells you no, ask what you’d need to hear yes, and write it down. Paper remembers what a hard week makes you forget.

You’re not behind. You’re at the part where it starts. Make the one call.
