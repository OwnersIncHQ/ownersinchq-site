The one check that matters most: decide how you'll make holes before you buy anything else. Power auger, drill-powered flight, or a hand auger — that one choice sets the budget for everything after it. Every card below carries our verdict: why we picked it, who it's for, who should skip it, and what to watch out for.

StrikeMaster Lithium 40V Maven Auger — 8"

The full-power electric: brushless motor, twin serrated stainless-steel blades, a built-in LED light and bubble level, forward and reverse — and TWO 40V batteries in the box (5Ah and 4Ah), so a dead pack never ends the day. Joe's lists it Fast & Free Shipping Eligible.

Why we picked it: It's the auger the whole category is measured against this season — real power, no gas, no pull cord, and a second battery included instead of sold separately.

Best for: Anglers who drill a lot of holes, move often, or fish thick mid-winter ice.

Skip if: You fish a handful of holes a few times a year — the drill unit and the hand auger below do that job for far less.

Watch out for: Blades are consumables: budget for a spare set, and keep the batteries warm — lithium loses punch in deep cold.

StrikeMaster Lite-Flite Lazer Drill Unit — 8"

Already own a good cordless drill? This is the flight that turns it into an ice auger: twin chrome-alloy stainless Lazer blades on a lightweight synthetic flight, with the blade guard and two-stage drill adapter in the box. Runs on a professional 18V (or higher) brushless drill with a 1/2" chuck.

Why we picked it: The cheapest honest route to power drilling — it borrows the drill already in your garage instead of selling you a second motor.

Best for: Run-and-gun anglers and anyone who already owns a strong brushless drill.

Skip if: Your drill is a small 12V or brushed model — it will struggle and drain fast. The Maven above is the self-contained answer.

Watch out for: Drill not included, and the price is a sale price — it was $242.99 on the same Joe's listing.

StrikeMaster Mora Hand Auger — 8"

The powder-blue classic that's been cutting Minnesota ice for decades. No battery, no motor, nothing to charge — just sharp blades, a wing-bolt, and a blade cover in the box. The auger that lives in the truck all winter as the backup that never fails.

Why we picked it: Every hardwater kit needs one tool with zero failure modes. This is it — and at this price it's the easiest first auger there is.

Best for: First-timers, panfish anglers drilling a few holes, and as the permanent backup next to a power auger.

Skip if: You're drilling through two feet of ice all day — your shoulders will file a complaint. Go power.

Watch out for: Hand-auger blades dull if they bang around loose; keep the cover on in transport.

Vexilar FLX-28 Genz Pack Flasher

The flasher serious ice anglers swear by: five-color display with digital depth, auto range, the Pro-View Ice-Ducer, and a sealed lead-acid battery in the pack. Scheels lists free shipping on this one — checked Nov 21, 2026.

Why we picked it: Instant, real-time sonar with no screen lag and no menu-diving at -10° — a flasher still beats a graph when the fish are tight to bottom.

Best for: Anglers who fish by the dial and want the top of Vexilar's flasher line without going to live sonar money.

Skip if: You want GPS mapping or a forward-facing live view — that's a different (much pricier) class of electronics.

Watch out for: This Genz Pack is the lead-acid battery version; Vexilar's lithium packs cost more. Confirm which battery you're buying.

Clam Yukon Thermal Flip-Over Shelter — 2-Angler

A two-angler flip-over with Clam's thermal (insulated, quilted) fabric: tow it behind the sled or ATV, flip it over, and you're fishing in a warm, dark house instead of kneeling in the wind. The shelter that turns a two-hour outing into an all-day one.

Why we picked it: Clam invented the flip-over, and the Yukon is its workhorse size — room for two anglers and a heater without going to a four-person sled.

Lakco Ice Safety Picks

Ten bucks of insurance that hangs around your neck: two spiked handles on a cord that give you something to dig in and pull with if you ever go through. The cheapest item on this page and the first one that should go in the bucket.

Why we picked it: Self-rescue picks are the one piece of ice gear nobody should skip — they weigh nothing, cost almost nothing, and only matter on the worst day.

Best for: Every single person who walks on ice. Early ice, late ice, any ice.

Skip if: Never skip these. If you already own a set, buy a set for whoever fishes with you.

Watch out for: Picks in a tackle box don't help — they only work worn around your neck, outside your coat.
